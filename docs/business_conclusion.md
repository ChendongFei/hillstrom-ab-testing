# Business conclusion

Both email assignments increased two-week purchase conversion relative to no email. Conversion rose from 0.573% in the control group to 1.253% for the Mens email and 0.884% for the Womens email, and both effects remain significant after Holm correction for the two comparisons. These are effects of being assigned an email in this historical sample; they hold under the design assumptions set out in the methodology (faithful randomization, complete outcome tracking, no interference between customers).

Exploratory revenue per assigned customer was about $0.77 higher for the Mens email (Welch 95% CI $0.49 to $1.05) and $0.42 higher for the Womens email ($0.17 to $0.68), in the source's currency units. Bootstrap intervals are similar. This is revenue, not profit, and the intervals are unadjusted. The two emails were each compared against control only; Mens was not tested directly against Womens, so these results do not rank the two creatives.

Whether a lift like this is worth acting on depends on margin and contact cost: incremental contribution per assigned customer = gross margin × incremental revenue − incremental contact cost. At an illustrative 40% margin and $0.05 contact cost, the point estimates imply about $0.26 (Mens) and $0.12 (Womens) per assigned customer. These figures are scenarios built on assumed inputs, not measured returns. The dataset contains no cost, margin, unsubscribe, or customer-fatigue data, and any of those could change the decision.

Recommendation: treat this as evidence that the emails worked in the 2008 sample, not as a rollout case today. Before acting on it, run a contemporary randomized validation with the primary metric, the minimum commercially meaningful effect, the two-week measurement window, and guardrail metrics (unsubscribes, complaints, downstream retention) specified in advance, and with stable customer IDs tracked end to end. If the goal is to choose between the two creatives, the direct Mens-vs-Womens comparison belongs in that experiment's prespecified analysis plan.
