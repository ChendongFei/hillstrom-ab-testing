# Business conclusion

Both email assignments show evidence of incremental two-week purchases compared with no email. Mens email raises conversion from 0.573% to 1.253%; Womens email to 0.884%. The primary results survive Holm correction. These are treatment-assignment effects in the historical sample, conditional on the design assumptions.

Exploratory incremental revenue per assigned customer is about $0.770 for Mens email (Welch 95% CI $0.485–$1.055) and $0.424 for Womens email ($0.169–$0.680), in source currency units—not CAD. Bootstrap intervals are similar. These are revenue, not profit; intervals are marginal and unadjusted. We did not test Mens directly against Womens, so we do not declare a winning creative.

A useful decision rule is incremental contribution = gross margin × incremental revenue − incremental contact cost. At an **illustrative**, unverified 40% margin and $0.05 contact cost, point estimates are $0.258 and $0.120 per assigned customer. These are scenarios, not measured ROI. Unsubscribes, customer fatigue and long-term value could change the decision.

Recommendation: use the findings to motivate a contemporary randomized validation. Specify the main conversion metric, commercial threshold, two-week maturation rule and guardrails before launch. Measure actual costs, delivery, opt-outs and downstream value; assign and track stable customer IDs. If choosing between creatives, include the direct comparison in the prespecified multiplicity plan.

For Canadian bank applications, the transferable skills are customer campaign measurement, holdout design, careful denominators, incremental uptake, business tradeoffs and auditability. This retail dataset cannot establish credit risk, banking profitability, regulatory compliance or outcomes for bank customers. A banking follow-up would need product-specific value and risk metrics plus customer-protection guardrails.
