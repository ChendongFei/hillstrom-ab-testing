# Analysis specification and statistical reasoning

## Context and estimand
The original source describes customers who had purchased in the preceding year, randomized roughly equally to a Mens email, a Womens email, or no email, with outcomes tracked for two weeks. The email names describe the creative content, not the customer's gender. The experimental unit is the customer as represented by one row in the extract; there is no customer ID to verify that one row really is one customer.

Primary outcome: any purchase within two weeks (`conversion`). Denominator: all assigned customers. The estimand is the intent-to-treat risk difference for each email vs no email. I don't restrict to visitors, purchasers, or delivered-email recipients — those are all post-assignment selections and conditioning on them would break the randomization. A causal reading needs consistency, no interference, independent units, faithful random assignment, and complete, nondifferential measurement. The extract can't verify those assumptions; it supports a causal reanalysis conditional on them.

H0 for each comparison: purchase probabilities are equal; H1: they differ. Two-sided alpha = 0.05 across the family of two primary tests. One caveat worth stating plainly: I wrote this specification after the outcomes were already accessible in the data. It documents the analysis, but it isn't a preregistration.

## Tests and uncertainty
- Pooled two-proportion z tests: randomized independent binary outcomes, sufficiently large successes/failures in each arm. The pooled variance corresponds to the null of equal probabilities. Tests explicitly handle all-zero/all-one totals. A unit test independently checks equivalence to Pearson's 2x2 chi-square test without continuity correction.
- Holm correction: controls family-wise error across the two primary comparisons, including their dependence through a shared control. No claim is based on cherry-picking unadjusted significance.
- Newcombe differences of Wilson intervals: marginal 95% uncertainty for absolute effects, avoiding the simplest Wald interval. Also report 97.5% intervals for two-comparison Bonferroni simultaneous coverage (approximate because component intervals are approximate). These are not exact inversions of Holm tests; report methods distinctly.
- Relative lift = (treatment/control)-1. It can appear large when baseline risk is low. Absolute percentage points and incremental purchases per 10,000 clarify scale. Confidence intervals focus on absolute effects.
- Revenue per assigned customer includes zeros (non-buyers are real outcomes, not missing data). Welch's t test and its 95% interval allow unequal variances and target a mean difference, justified by the large samples rather than by any assumption that individual spend is normal. A seeded nonparametric bootstrap (3,000 replicates, percentile interval) checks sensitivity to the heavy skew; resampling counts over unique values is mathematically the same as resampling rows for this statistic. The bootstrap doesn't fix missing customers, confounding, or dependence. Revenue is exploratory and unadjusted — it isn't a second confirmatory family. Visit rates are descriptive only.
- Do not use a normality-test gatekeeper to switch automatically to Mann–Whitney: that would change the estimand away from mean revenue. Do not remove valid high spend simply because it is extreme.

## Quality and experiment checks
Validate exact schema, missingness, binary support, finite nonnegative monetary values, recency 1–12, all expected arms, and outcome coherence (purchase implies visit and positive spend matches conversion). Fail loudly on violations. Preserve exact-profile repeats because true duplicate customer identities cannot be established. Source SHA-256 fixes the analysis input. Cleaning is limited to a documented location-label typo.

SRM uses a Pearson goodness-of-fit chi-square statistic with two degrees of freedom and expected probabilities of 1/3 each, following the original description. The actual assignment implementation isn't available; if fixed quotas or stratification were used, this multinomial diagnostic is only approximate. I use p < 0.001 as an investigation flag — a larger p-value isn't proof that randomization worked. No exclusions are made based on SRM.

Baseline balance uses standardized mean differences for history, recency, purchase flags, new-customer flag, and one-hot channel/location. It is descriptive; a reference magnitude of 0.1 is an investigation aid, not a formal validation criterion. `history_segment` is redundant with continuous history. Never adjust for post-treatment spend/visit when estimating the conversion effect.

## Power and decision relevance
Power tables are future-design sensitivity analyses, not observed post-hoc power. Use the observed control rate as an explicitly uncertain planning baseline, equal group sizes, 80% power and conservative per-comparison alpha=0.025. The normal approximation with Cohen's arcsine h gives a positive-direction MDE of about 0.247 percentage points at 21,306 per arm. This does not prove the original experiment was adequately planned. For example, detecting a 0.10 pp increase needs approximately 117,433 per arm under these assumptions. Future studies should vary baseline assumptions, specify a commercially meaningful effect and allow for tracking losses.

No minimum business-worthy effect is invented for the historical campaign, because the data needed to set one (margins, contact costs) isn't in the extract. Statistical significance alone can't establish value. And a nonsignificant comparison wouldn't establish equivalence either.

## Unresolved issues
No IDs or assignment/ingestion logs to verify independence, duplicate identity, attrition, or exclusions; no household clusters; no timestamps to check concurrent operation or peeking; no original stopping rule or predeclared primary outcome; the source-era retail sample limits transportability; no deliverability or spillover measurement; sparse purchases constrain subgroup precision; no experimental costs, profit margins, or customer-harm guardrails. External replication and a properly prespecified new experiment are still needed. Targeting/uplift modelling is out of scope for now — it only makes sense once this baseline is understood and an honest holdout evaluation can be designed for it.
